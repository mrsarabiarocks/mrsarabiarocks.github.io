Prompt 01

Lesson and unit planning

Start from the end and work backward. Claude drafts the plan, you keep the judgment. Build faster so you can teach better.

Backwards-design a unit

When you have a text or topic and a blank calendar staring at you.

You are helping me plan a unit using backwards design. I teach [grade] [subject]. The unit is on [text or topic] and I have [number] class periods of [length] minutes. Start with the end: give me 3 possible summative assessments students could do to show mastery. For each, tell me what standard or skill it proves. Then, once I pick one, we will map the daily lessons backward from it. Ask me which assessment I want before you build the calendar. Keep it real for middle schoolers. No busywork. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.
Get my step-by-step guide here!Watch: Backwards planning with Claude

Turn a standard into a lesson

When you know the standard but not the lesson.

I teach [grade] [subject]. Build me a single [length]-minute lesson that hits this standard: [paste standard]. Give me: a hook that takes 5 minutes or less, the main activity, one check for understanding, and an exit ticket. Tell me exactly what I say and what students do. Assume I have zero prep time and a class that talks a lot. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Prompt 02

Differentiation

One lesson, three readiness levels, five minutes. This is the one that saves your prep period.

Scaffolds for the kids who need them

When you have IEPs, ELLs, and everyone in between.

I am teaching this assignment to [grade]: [paste assignment]. Give me a support version for: (1) students who need reading support, (2) English language learners, and (3) students who finish early and need a challenge. For each, give me the actual modified directions and any sentence stems or word banks. Keep the learning goal identical for all of them. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.
See it in action

Three readiness levels of the same text

When one reading is too hard for half the room and too easy for the other half.

Here is a reading passage for my [grade] [subject] class: [paste passage]. Rewrite it at 3 readiness levels while keeping the same key ideas and vocabulary goals: 1. Below grade level (shorter sentences, more scaffolding) 2. On grade level 3. Above grade level (more complex text, a stretch question) For each version, add 2 comprehension questions and 1 discussion question. Do not water down the thinking, just the reading load. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Prompt 03

Parent and admin emails

The email you have been avoiding since Tuesday. Draft it in your voice, kind but firm, in 30 seconds.

The tricky parent email

When you need to be honest without starting a war.

Help me write an email to a parent. Here is the situation: [describe what happened, plain and honest]. Tone: warm, professional, and clear. I am not apologizing for holding a standard, but I want the parent on my team. Keep it short. Lead with something positive about their kid if there is one. End with a specific next step. Give me two versions: one softer, one more direct. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.
Claude tips

Newsletter or admin update in your voice

When you have to write it and would rather do literally anything else.

Write a [class newsletter / update to my admin] covering these points: [dump your bullet points here, messy is fine]. My voice: real, warm, a little dry, no corporate buzzwords, no exclamation-point overload. Short paragraphs. Make it skimmable. Then give me a subject line that people will actually open. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Prompt 04

PBL and creative projects

This is my whole thing. Take any text or topic and turn it into a project kids actually want to do.

Remix a lesson into a pop-culture project

Shark Tank, Mythbusters, a newscast, a meme. Same standards, way more buy-in.

I teach [grade] [subject]. We are studying [text or topic]. I want to turn it into a project modeled on [Shark Tank / Mythbusters / a news broadcast / a social media takeover / your idea]. Give me: the project pitch in kid-friendly language, the steps students follow, what they produce, and a simple rubric tied to [standard or skill]. Keep it doable in [number] class periods. Make it fun but rigorous. I do not do busywork projects. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Build an annotation set from any text

Speeches, primary sources, song lyrics, a chapter. Close reading with a purpose.

Here is a text I want my [grade] students to annotate closely: [paste text or name it]. Give me 6 to 8 annotation prompts that push real thinking (tone, word choice, author's purpose, connections to now). Write them as questions students answer in the margins. Then give me one short project they do after annotating to show they got it. Balance matters to me, so make at least part of this something they do on paper, by hand. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Prompt 05

Workshop and station boards

Run stations without repeating directions 40 times. One board on the screen shows every group where to go, what to do, and how much time is left.

Build a reading workshop board

When you have color groups rotating through stations and want everything on one screen.

Build me a reading workshop board for my [grade] class as a single web page I can put on my classroom screen. The text is [text or topic]. My groups are [Blue, Red, Orange, Green] and they rotate through [number] blocks. The stations are: [station 1 and where it is], [station 2 and where it is], [station 3 and where it is]. One station is a teacher meeting with me. Include: a schedule tab showing which station each group is at during each block, a directions tab with numbered steps, an example, and a sentence starter for every station, what to bring, a big countdown timer I can start and reset, and a points tracker for each group. Make it look like [theme, like a spooky Poe vibe], easy to read from the back of the room, and fun. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Swap in your own stations. Mine have included an escape room, a choice board, and a teacher meeting.

Mastering Claude and Bulk Create

The move that made me go viral: build the content once with Claude, then let Canva's Bulk Create spin up a whole year of slides in one go. This is how you make 180 things without making 180 things.

Fill a Canva Bulk Create template

Agenda slides, vocab cards, bell ringers, task cards. Anything you'd make one at a time.

I want to mass-produce [180 daily agenda slides / vocab cards / bell ringers] in Canva using Bulk Create. First ask me what fields my template has (like Date, Day, Objective, Agenda, Homework). Then generate a clean table I can paste straight into Canva Bulk Create: one row per slide, columns matching my fields exactly. Fill it with [my topics and pacing], keep it [grade] appropriate, and do not skip days. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Claude Advanced

Ready to stop copying prompts one at a time? This is where Claude goes from a helper to a system that knows your classroom. Three features, three unfair advantages: Projects, Skills, and Cowork.

See one I actually built

The Outsiders study tool, for my real students

I had Claude build a student-facing website for our Outsiders unit. It coaches kids through theme, motif, conflict, plot diagram, and symbolism, giving sentence stems and fill-in-the-blanks, then rewriting their attempts when they are close. I uploaded our actual class materials and gave it rules for how to talk to them, so they are not pulling random stuff off the internet. It refuses anything off-topic, and yes, it has a 1960s vibe.

Open the live tool
See the exact prompt I gave Claude (swap in your own novel)
Build me an AI study tool for my students for the novel [The Outsiders]. Cover five sections: theme, motif, conflict, plot diagram, and symbolism. Students are middle school (6th to 8th grade). Give partial answers with sentence stems and blanks to fill in, and offer a rewrite when they are a little off. Every section requires students to include a quote as support, then write their own analysis, symbolism, or conflict statement. THEME: Guide them to a universal, non-cliche theme statement (no character names, no plot summary) using the "the author wants us to know..." frame, landing on the big lesson. Focus the theme on being true to yourself and knowing your true identity, inspired by Johnny's line "stay gold." Model statement: The innocence of youth is something we should cherish because nothing stays gold forever, and staying true to yourself as you grow and change helps guide your identity and choices. MOTIF: Focus on the motif of literature and how it recurs to build the identity theme, using three moments: the poem "Nothing Gold Can Stay," Johnny and Ponyboy reading Gone with the Wind in the church, and the reveal that Ponyboy is the author. Model statement: The motif of literature is present when Ponyboy recites Robert Frost's "Nothing Gold Can Stay," when Ponyboy and Johnny read Gone with the Wind together in the church, and when Ponyboy reveals he is the author telling the story. These moments remind me that embracing your true identity instead of the labels society gives you helps guide your choices and lets you stay true to yourself. CONFLICT: Main conflict should be person vs. self or person vs. society. RULES: Refuse to answer anything not related to The Outsiders. Fun, engaging, student-friendly design with a 1960s Outsiders vibe.

Advanced 01: Projects

Build a Claude that already knows your classroom

New to Projects? A Project is a saved workspace where you upload your files and set your instructions once. Everything you do inside it remembers your grade, your standards, and your voice. Set it up on a Sunday, save hours every week after.

Your "Classroom HQ" project

Why it's powerful: Stop re-typing your grade, standards, and style every single time. Set it once and every lesson comes out aligned and sounding like you.

Starter setup

Create a new Project called "Classroom HQ." Upload your pacing guide, your standards, one rubric, and one lesson that sounds like you. Then paste this into the project's custom instructions: You are my teaching assistant for [grade] [subject]. Everything you make should align to the standards and pacing guide I uploaded and match the voice in my sample lesson: real, clear, a little dry, no busywork. Default to middle school reading levels. When I ask for a lesson or activity, tie it to a standard and keep prep low. Ask me the topic and how many days I have, then build. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.
Claude tips

A project per novel or unit

Why it's powerful: Reuse everything you built for The Outsiders, Poe, or Night without rebuilding it. The project becomes your unit's memory.

Starter setup

Make a Project named for the text (e.g. "The Outsiders Unit"). Upload the reading, your annotation sets, and past projects. Paste into custom instructions: This project holds everything for my [text] unit with [grade]. Treat the uploaded reading, annotations, and projects as the source of truth. When I ask for something new (a quiz, a bell ringer, a differentiated version, a project), match the style and rigor of what's already here and keep it aligned to [standard]. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Your grading and feedback partner

Why it's powerful: Feedback that matches your rubric and still sounds like you, in a fraction of the time. You stay the teacher, it drafts the words.

Starter setup

Create a Project called "Feedback." Upload your rubric and 2 to 3 samples of feedback you have written. Paste into custom instructions: You help me draft feedback on student work for [grade] [subject]. Always score against the uploaded rubric and explain it in kid-friendly language. Match the tone of my samples: warm, specific, honest, growth-focused. Give 2 glows and 1 grow. Never rewrite the student's work for them. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Keep it FERPA-safe: strip student names or use initials before you paste any work.

Advanced 02: Skills

Teach Claude your signature moves

New to Skills? A Skill is a set of instructions you save once and trigger by name. It is how you teach Claude a move you make all the time, so you never re-type the directions again. Build the skill once, call it forever.

The PBL Remix Machine

Why it's powerful: Turn any text or topic into a project kids beg to do, on command. This is my whole brand in one saved skill.

Skill instructions to save

When I give you a text or topic and a format (Shark Tank, Mythbusters, a newscast, a social media takeover, a meme), turn it into a student project. Always output: a kid-friendly pitch, numbered student steps, what they turn in, and a simple 4-row rubric tied to the standard I name. Middle school level. Fun but rigorous. No busywork. If I do not give a format, suggest three. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Advanced 03: Cowork

Put Claude to work on your actual desktop

New to Cowork? Cowork is Claude on your computer. It can open your files, organize folders, build things, and even run tasks on a schedule. This is where Claude stops giving advice and starts doing the work.

Clean up my Drive

Why it's powerful: Turn a chaotic resource folder into something you can actually find things in. I did exactly this with my whole PBL Drive.

Say this in Cowork

Connect to my Google Drive and audit my [folder name] folder. Give me a full inventory organized by subfolder, flag duplicates and misfiled items, and suggest a cleaner structure. Do not move anything until I approve it. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

Build me a classroom tool

Why it's powerful: Interactive resources built for you, not bought. This whole hub was made with Claude.

Say this in Cowork

Build me an interactive [choice board / review game / student hub] for [grade] on [topic]. Make it a single web page, mobile-friendly, in my brand colors. Include [what you want on it]. Then give me the file so I can share or host it. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.

My Monday "week ahead"

Why it's powerful: Start the week already organized without lifting a finger. Set it once and it runs itself.

Say this in Cowork

Every Monday at 6am, pull my calendar for the week and my to-do list, and send me a short "week ahead" brief: what is coming, what I need to prep, and the top 3 things to knock out first. Keep it skimmable. Ask me some questions to make sure I get the best output possible.
